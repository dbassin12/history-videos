Did Napoleon save the Revolution? Or did he kill it?

What happens

  1. 1769Napoleon Bonaparte is born on Corsica, an island France had just taken over.
  2. December 1793At 24, his cannons help drive the British out of Toulon, and he is made a general.
  3. October 5, 1795He fires grapeshot into royalists marching on the government in Paris. About 300 are killed. A few weeks later, the Directory takes over.
  4. 1796 to 1797He conquers northern Italy and ships home gold and art.
  5. 1798 to 1799He invades Egypt. The British destroy his fleet at the Battle of the Nile. At Jaffa his men kill more than 2,000 prisoners. With his army stuck in Egypt, he leaves it and sails home.
  6. November 9, 1799Sieyès and Napoleon invent a plot and move the lawmakers to Saint-Cloud.
  7. November 10, 1799The lawmakers mob Napoleon. Lucien tells the soldiers the lawmakers pulled daggers. Grenadiers clear the hall. That night a few dozen lawmakers name three consuls: Napoleon, Sieyès and Ducos.
  8. December 1799 to February 1800A new constitution makes Napoleon First Consul, and Sieyès and Ducos are pushed aside. A plebiscite approves it, with about half of the yes votes made up.
  9. 180060 of Paris's 73 political newspapers are shut down.
  10. March 21, 1804The Duke of Enghien, a royal prince, is kidnapped and shot at Vincennes.
  11. March 1804The Napoleonic Code becomes law.
  12. December 2, 1804Napoleon crowns himself Emperor in Notre-Dame, with the pope watching.
  13. 1806 to 1807Napoleon calls an Assembly of Jewish Notables and a Grand Sanhedrin in Paris. It promises that Jews are loyal Frenchmen.
  14. March 1808The "Infamous Decree" limits Jewish moneylending and where Jews can live, for ten years.

Why it matters

  • Equality, but no liberty: the press was silenced
  • One code of laws for all of France (the Napoleonic Code)
  • Ghettos opened in conquered lands; Jews in France asked to prove their loyalty, then limited by the 1808 decree
  • Louisiana sold to the U.S. in 1803, doubling its size
  • The Revolution's ideas spread across Europe by war
  • Between 3 and 6 million dead in his wars

Words to know

Hors la loi (outlaw)
Declared outside the law's protection, so anyone could kill the person without a trial.
Coup (coup d'état)
A sudden, illegal grab for power, usually with the help of soldiers.
Grapeshot
A cannon load of small iron balls that sprays out like a giant shotgun.
The Directory
The government of France from 1795 to 1799, led by five directors.
Consul
One of the three men who ran France after the coup (a title from ancient Rome). From December 1799 the First Consul, Napoleon, held almost all the power.
Plebiscite
A yes-or-no vote by the people on one question.
Napoleonic Code
The single set of laws Napoleon gave France in 1804: equality before the law for men, but wives under their husbands.
Grand Sanhedrin
A council of 71 rabbis and Jewish leaders that Napoleon called in Paris in 1807.
Emperor
A ruler of an empire. Napoleon crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804.
