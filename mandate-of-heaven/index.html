Ancient China · Classical civilizations

The Mandate of Heaven

China, 1046 BCE to 1912 CE · a narrated documentary, 5:16 · captions on screen

The question it ends on

“Was it a real check on bad rulers, or a way to make any victory look like Heaven's will?”

The idea in five lines

  • Heaven gives a ruler the right to rule
  • A good ruler keeps the Mandate
  • A cruel or weak ruler loses it
  • Floods, famines, earthquakes and rebellions are the signs
  • A new dynasty claims the Mandate, and the cycle starts again

Teacher notes

  • The Qin lateness law is a good discussion point. Sima Qian says being late meant death, but Qin law texts found at Shuihudi in 1975 show scoldings and fines for lateness, and duty canceled for heavy rain. Some historians think Chen Sheng's guard duty fell under harsher military rules. Sima Qian wrote under the Han, the dynasty that replaced the Qin: the winners again.
  • The 260 BCE map shows a small state called Han. It is not the Han dynasty, which began in 206 BCE.
  • Two deaths by suicide are mentioned (the last Shang king and the last Ming emperor). Neither is shown.
  • The battle drums are Japanese taiko recordings, used as stand-in war drums; the other instruments are Chinese (guqin, guzheng, erhu, gong, a Suzhou drum).

What happens

  1. c.1600–1046 BCEThe Shang dynasty rules the Yellow River valley. Its kings ask their ancestors for advice with oracle bones.
  2. 1046 BCEKing Wu of Zhou defeats the last Shang king at Muye. The Zhou explain their victory with the Mandate of Heaven.
  3. c.372–289 BCEMencius, a follower of Confucius, teaches that Heaven sees and hears through the people.
  4. 221 BCEThe king of Qin conquers the other Warring States and becomes the First Emperor. He rules by Legalism.
  5. 209 BCEDrafted farmers led by Chen Sheng rebel against the Qin.
  6. 206–202 BCEThe Qin falls. Liu Bang, a commoner, founds the Han dynasty.
  7. 184 CEThe Yellow Turban Rebellion. The generals who crush it become warlords.
  8. 220 CEThe Han ends. China stays divided until 589.
  9. 1644Rebels take Beijing and the last Ming emperor dies. The Manchus take Beijing and rule as the Qing.
  10. 1912The last Qing emperor gives up the throne. China's dynasties end.

The voices and their sources

  • King Wu of Zhou: Book of Documents, “Speech at Mu” (adapted)
  • The Duke of Zhou: Book of Documents, “Announcement to Kang” (traditionally his words)
  • Mencius: The Mencius, quoting the Book of Documents
  • Qin Shi Huangdi: Sima Qian, Records of the Grand Historian
  • Chen Sheng: Sima Qian, Records of the Grand Historian
  • The Yellow Turbans: their slogan, in the Book of the Later Han
  • Prince Dorgon: dramatized: not his exact words

Words to know

Mandate of Heaven
The belief that Heaven gives a ruler the right to rule, but only as long as he rules well.
Heaven (Tian)
To the Zhou, the highest power above. Not a place.
Dynasty
A family that rules a country, passing power down for generations.
Dynastic cycle
The pattern of a dynasty's rise, golden age, decline and collapse, then a new dynasty.
Oracle bones
Turtle shells and cattle bones the Shang heated until they cracked, to ask their ancestors questions.
Confucianism
The teachings of Confucius: rulers should be good examples and care for the people. Mencius was his most famous follower.
Legalism
Keep order with strict laws and harsh punishments. People obey out of fear.
Yellow Turban Rebellion
A huge peasant uprising against the Han in 184 CE.
Manchus
A people from the northeast who conquered China in 1644 and ruled as the Qing dynasty.
