The question it ends on

“Did the Mandate of Heaven really stop bad rulers? Or did it just make any victory look like Heaven’s will?”

The cycle on screen, numbered like the worksheet

  1. Steps 1, 12
    The Mandate
    A new emperor gains the Mandate of Heaven.
  2. Steps 2, 3
    Prosperity
    Peace, more food, more people.
  3. Steps 4 to 6
    Decline
    Corruption, natural disaster, famine, revolt.
  4. Steps 7 to 9
    Chaos
    The emperor loses the Mandate. War, the population falls, and no one is in control.
  5. Steps 10, 11
    A new dynasty
    One state wins and starts a new dynasty.

Teacher notes

  • Where to pause. The video runs 10:10. The start of Chapter 3 (5:52) splits it into two halves: the Mandate and the whole cycle first (about 6 minutes), then the Qin, the Han and the last dynasties (about 4 minutes).
  • The Qin and the Mandate. The First Emperor's own stone inscriptions never mention Heaven's support, so the video says the Qin “united China” and has the Han claim the Mandate. Good question for class: why would a ruler skip the Mandate? (Yuri Pines, The Messianic Emperor.)
  • Late meant death?. Sima Qian says it did. Qin law texts found at Shuihudi in 1975 show fines for lateness and duty canceled in heavy rain. Sima Qian wrote under the Han, the dynasty that replaced the Qin.
  • Dates. The video uses 1046 BCE for Muye, the most cited date. The worksheet says 1045, which some scholars prefer. The Warring States ran about 475 to 221 BCE; the worksheet's 770 to 453 BCE describes the Spring and Autumn period.
  • Confucius and Mencius. Confucius died in 479 BCE, before the Warring States began. Mencius (about 372 to 289 BCE) was a later follower.
  • Two Hans. On the Warring States map, “Han” is the kingdom of Han (韓), one of the seven states. It is not the later Han dynasty (漢), which took its name from the Han River valley where Liu Bang first ruled. Worth pointing out in class.
  • Mencius's words. Legge translates “Heaven sees as my people see.” The video says “the people” because Mencius is quoting an older text, not speaking as a ruler.
  • The small fish. “Ruling a big country is like cooking a small fish” comes from the Daodejing (chapter 60), the classic Daoist text credited to Laozi.
  • The first Great Wall. The Qin joined older walls built by the warring states. Most of it was rammed earth; the stone walls visitors see today were built much later, mostly under the Ming.
  • Puyi's age. Puyi was born in February 1906 and gave up the throne on 12 February 1912, days after turning six. The photo on screen is from 1909, when he was three.
  • The drums. The battle drums are Japanese taiko recordings, used as stand-in war drums. The guqin, guzheng, erhu, gong and Suzhou drum are Chinese.

Words to know

Heaven天
A great power that decides the fate of people and kings. Not a place you go when you die.
Dynasty朝
A ruling family that holds power for generations.
Mandate of Heaven天命
Heaven's permission for one dynasty to rule China, as long as it rules well.
Son of Heaven天子
Heaven's chosen ruler on earth: the title of the Zhou kings and later emperors.
Dynastic cycle
The pattern of a new dynasty, good times, decline, chaos, and a new dynasty again.
Lords
Nobles who ruled their own regions for the Zhou kings.
Warring States戰國
About 250 years of war among seven kingdoms, ending in 221 BCE.
Hundred Schools of Thought百家
The many thinkers with answers to how to bring back order.
Confucianism儒家
Rulers should lead by good example, and everyone has duties to others.
Daoism道家
Live simply, in harmony with nature, and rule as little as possible. Dao means the Way.
Legalism法家
Control people with strict laws, harsh punishments and rewards for obeying.
First Emperor始皇帝
Qin Shi Huangdi, the Qin king who united China in 221 BCE and took a bigger title.
Han Chinese漢族
The name most people in China use for themselves today, taken from the Han dynasty.
Yellow Turbans黃巾
A huge peasant rebellion against the Han in 184 CE.
Blue Heaven, Yellow Heaven蒼天 黃天
In the Yellow Turbans' slogan, the Blue Heaven stood for the Han and the Yellow Heaven for the rebels.
Manchus滿洲
A people from beyond the Great Wall who took over China in 1644 and ruled as the Qing.
Qing清
China's last dynasty, 1644 to 1912.
