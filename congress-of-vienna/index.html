The Congress of Vienna

Rebuilding Europe, 1814–1815 · a narrated documentary with the map before and after, 5:59 · captions on screen

How it ends: the summary

  • It brought back the old kings (legitimacy)
  • It boxed in France with strong neighbors
  • It redrew the map for a balance of power
  • It created the Concert of Europe
  • It ignored nationalism, which would explode in 1848

The result: no war would drag in all of Europe's great powers for 99 years, until 1914. Smaller wars still happened, like the Crimean War.

What happens

  1. 1792–1814More than twenty years of war across Europe. Millions die.
  2. April 1814Napoleon gives up his throne and is sent to the island of Elba. Louis XVIII becomes king of France.
  3. Fall 1814Kings, emperors and diplomats gather in Vienna. Five great powers make the real decisions.
  4. Winter 1814–15Russia wants nearly all of Poland and Prussia wants all of Saxony. Austria, Britain and France refuse.
  5. January 3, 1815Britain, Austria and France sign a secret treaty against Russia and Prussia.
  6. February 1815The compromise: Russia gets most of Poland; Prussia gets part of Saxony and the Rhineland.
  7. March to June 1815Napoleon escapes from Elba. The Congress signs its Final Act on June 9. Napoleon is beaten at Waterloo on June 18.
  8. September 1815Russia, Austria and Prussia form the Holy Alliance.
  9. November 1815The great powers promise to keep meeting: the start of the Concert of Europe.
  10. 1820–1823Revolts in Italy and Spain are crushed by foreign armies.
  11. 1848Revolutions explode across Europe. Metternich flees Vienna.
  12. 1859–1871Italy and Germany unite through war. Germany becomes the strongest power on the continent.
  13. 1914Europe is split into two armed camps. World War I is the first war since 1815 to drag in all of Europe's great powers.

The five players

  • Metternich (Austria): kings, order, and no more revolutions
  • Castlereagh (Britain): a balance, so no one country can rule Europe
  • Alexander I (Russia): Poland
  • Hardenberg (Prussia): the rich kingdom of Saxony
  • Talleyrand (France): a seat at the table for the country that lost

In the video each man speaks in his own voice. Most of their lines are dramatized and marked that way on screen. Castlereagh's "sublime mysticism and nonsense" is his real phrase, and Talleyrand's "Allies? And against whom?" comes from his own memoirs.

Words to know

Legitimacy
The idea that the old royal families are the rightful rulers and should get their thrones back.
Balance of power
No single country may grow strong enough to dominate the rest; together, the others must always be stronger.
Containment
Surrounding a country with strong neighbors so it cannot break out and attack again.
Compensation
If one great power gains land, the others must gain too, so the balance stays even.
Concert of Europe
After the Congress, the great powers keep meeting to settle problems and keep the peace.
Holy Alliance
Russia, Austria and Prussia promise to rule by Christian principles and help each other. Britain refuses to join.
Nationalism
The belief that a people who share a language and culture should rule themselves.
Conservative
Wanting to keep the old order: kings, nobles and the church, and no revolutions.
