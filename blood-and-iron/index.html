Blood and IronThe Unification of Germany, 1815–1871

A 13:09 documentary for Global History 10: nationalism, 1848, Bismarck's three wars, and how a new Germany broke the balance of power.

The question it ends on

Bismarck built Germany with blood and iron. Can a country built that way ever live in peace?

What happens

  • 1815 After Napoleon, the Congress of Vienna creates the German Confederation of 39 states.
  • 1848 Revolutions sweep Europe. The Frankfurt Parliament tries to unite Germany.
  • 1849 The King of Prussia refuses the crown. The revolutions are crushed.
  • 1862 Bismarck becomes Prussia's chief minister: "iron and blood."
  • 1864 Level 1: Prussia and Austria together beat Denmark (Dybbøl, 18 April).
  • 1866 Level 2: Prussia turns on Austria and wins in seven weeks (Königgrätz, 3 July).
  • 1867 The North German Confederation, led by Prussia.
  • 1870 The Ems Telegram (13 July). France declares war (19 July). Napoleon III captured at Sedan (2 September).
  • 1871 The German Empire is proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles (18 January). France loses Alsace and part of Lorraine.
  • 1914 Europe, split into two armed camps, goes to war.
  • 1919 Germany signs the peace treaty in the same Hall of Mirrors.

Why it matters

  • A new great power appeared in the middle of Europe: more people than France, the strongest army, the fastest-growing factories.
  • The balance of power set up in 1815 was broken. Disraeli: "The balance of power has been entirely destroyed."
  • France wanted revenge for Alsace-Lorraine, and Europe split into alliances that went to war in 1914.
  • Germany was united from the top down by war, not by votes, the opposite of what the people of 1848 had wanted.

Words to know

Nationalism
The belief that people who share a language, a history and a culture are one nation, and should have one country.
Self-determination
The idea that a people should choose its own government, instead of kings or foreign powers choosing for them.
German Confederation
The loose club of 39 German states set up in 1815. Each kept its own ruler, army, money and border.
Constitution
Written rules that limit what a ruler can do.
Frankfurt Parliament
The elected assembly of 1848-49 that tried to write a constitution for one united Germany.
Big Germany / Small Germany
Big Germany meant a united Germany with Catholic Austria in it and in charge. Small Germany meant Austria out and Protestant Prussia in charge.
Junker
A Prussian noble from a family of big landowners.
Blood and iron
Bismarck's 1862 phrase: Germany would be united by war (blood) and weapons and industry (iron), not by speeches and votes.
Realpolitik
The politics of reality: do whatever makes your country stronger, even if it means breaking promises or turning on an ally.
Needle gun
Prussia's rifle loaded from the back, so soldiers could fire lying down, several times faster than an enemy loading from the front.
Kriegsspiel
German for "war game": a map-table game the Prussian army used to train its officers.
Duchy
A land ruled by a duke (Schleswig and Holstein).
North German Confederation
The union of 22 northern German states led by Prussia after 1866: one army under the Prussian king, with Bismarck as chancellor.
Ems Telegram
The 1870 telegram Bismarck shortened so it sounded like an insult to France, which helped start the Franco-Prussian War.
Kaiser
German for emperor, from the Roman name Caesar.
Reich
German for empire. The German Empire of 1871 was the German Reich.
Chancellor
The emperor's chief minister, who ran the government.
Revanche
French for revenge: what France wanted after losing Alsace and Lorraine.
Balance of power
The idea that no one country should be strong enough to bully the others.
