The Storming of the Bastille

July 14, 1789 · a narrated documentary, 3:17 · captions on screen

The question it ends on

Was July 14 a victory for freedom? Or the day the Revolution learned that violence works?

What happens

  1. June 20The new National Assembly swears the Tennis Court Oath.
  2. July 11Louis XVI fires Necker, the popular finance minister.
  3. July 12Camille Desmoulins calls Parisians to arms at the Palais-Royal.
  4. July 14, morningThe crowd takes about 30,000 muskets from the Invalides, but no gunpowder.
  5. About 1:30 PMAttackers cut the drawbridge chains and flood into the Bastille's courtyard. The defenders open fire.
  6. After 3 PMFrench Guards, royal soldiers who joined the crowd, aim cannons at the gate.
  7. About 5 PMGovernor de Launay surrenders. 98 attackers have died. The crowd kills de Launay and parades his head on a pike.
  8. July 15 to 17The king sends his troops away, recalls Necker and comes to Paris.
  9. 1789 to 1790Workers tear the Bastille down. Lafayette sends its main key to George Washington.
  10. 1880July 14 becomes France's national holiday.

Why it matters

  • The king backed down
  • The National Assembly was safe
  • The Bastille was torn down
  • July 14: national holiday since 1880
  • A darker lesson: a crowd with weapons can beat a king

Words to know

Lettre de cachet
A sealed order signed by the king that could send someone to prison with no trial.
National Assembly
The new assembly of representatives that the Third Estate formed in June 1789.
French Guards
Royal soldiers stationed in Paris. On July 14 many joined the crowd and brought cannons.
Les Invalides
A Paris hospital and home for old soldiers, with an armory.
